# Circuit evidence audit

Review date: 29 October 2026.

## Scope and status

This targeted audit covers all 29 guided circuits and their 101 authored landmarks. It checks citation identity, clinical-source traceability, anatomical naming, laterality and the distinction between circuit background and deficit evidence. It is not a systematic review, independent clinical sign-off or validation of patient-level diagnosis, prognosis or treatment decisions.

All 29 tours now declare their clinical sources separately from circuit background. Together they cite 94 references, including 49 references assigned a deficit-evidence role. Each tour must cite at least one work already recorded as supporting its linked deficit in the clinical catalogue. This verifies provenance, not the strength or universality of the biological association.

The wider clinical catalogue contains 70 profiles, 108 associations and 131 references. Its recorded extraction scope is 88 abstracts and 43 full texts. These counts describe extraction records, not a claim that every full text was independently re-reviewed in this audit. The [full-text review log](full-text-review.md) records the eight articles reviewed in this pass, source locations, substantive corrections and unreviewed material.

## Material findings and corrections

- The visual chiasm is an anatomical starting reference; homonymous field loss concerns retrochiasmal injury. Visual-field shape does not uniquely locate the lesion.
- The memory tour does not reproduce the bilateral hippocampal or fornical injuries in its patient sources. Mammillary degeneration alone did not distinguish amnesic from non-amnesic Wernicke cases in Harding’s study.
- The basal-ganglia schematic omits the substantia nigra. Nigral degeneration in Parkinson’s disease and uncommon parkinsonism after focal lentiform injury must not be treated as one interchangeable lesion mechanism.
- The hearing tour’s dorsal medial geniculate solid is explicitly a parallel comparison, not the ventral relay to A1. Bilateral cortical and selected unilateral cortical/white-matter word-deafness cases do not establish one necessary lesion pattern.
- Somatosensory deficit evidence concerns arm/hand tests and opercular/white-matter lesions; whole thalamic and cortical reference parcels are not exact patient lesions.
- Cerebellar ataxia evidence includes a mixed ischemic/surgical cohort and patients operated in childhood or adolescence; it must not be reported as 90 adult strokes.
- Praxis wording now identifies the Grasp/Use framework as a teaching model, includes qualifying imitation findings and avoids assigning optic ataxia to one atlas MIP parcel or treating one human–monkey contrast as universal species exclusivity.
- Unilateral motion-blindness and partial Bálint-syndrome cases qualify classic bilateral examples. They do not isolate a specific atlas parcel or establish identical mechanisms across cases.
- The achromatopsia meta-analysis’s published correction is displayed with its DOI and bilingual summary. Healthy-observer colour decoding is not a clinical lesion map.
- Intraparietal numeracy evidence is task dependent: the recent lesion-case review concerns approximation, rather than every numerical ability.
- The six recent additions retain only lesion-supported clinical comparisons. Broad parcels, small series, selected tasks and unresolved speech-apraxia localization remain explicitly limited.

## What automated checks establish

- Every circuit, source identifier, bilingual field, region label and supported view resolves against the published data.
- Clinical-source roles are nonempty, distinct and a subset of a tour’s citations; every tour directly cites supporting evidence for its linked deficit.
- Titles, identifiers, first authors, issue years and listed corrections agree with the cached PubMed/Bookshelf/Crossref records. Recorded retractions or expressions of concern fail the reference checks.
- Published correction notices require a valid DOI and English/French summaries and are visible in the circuit Sources panel.
- Browser checks exercise the source roles, correction notice and visible validation limit in both languages, alongside tour navigation and accessibility checks.

These checks do not establish patient-level predictive accuracy. In particular, sharing an atlas parcel, functional activation or a normative network connection does not prove direct injury, a necessary/sufficient site or a measured connection in this reference individual.

## Clinical-use work that remains

The published anatomy reports `clinical_accuracy_validated: false`. No independent clinician sign-off or patient-level validation is recorded by this audit. The supported use remains research and education, including reference use by clinicians; this audit does not expand it to individual clinical decisions.

Any proposed patient-specific use needs a defined intended use and patient population, independent specialist review of the relevant full texts and mappings, and evaluation against appropriate patient data with predefined performance and error analyses. The 88 abstract-based extraction records remain a concrete full-text review backlog. A catalogue-wide declaration that all deficit pathways are established would be unsupported.
